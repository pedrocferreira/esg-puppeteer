Amendment to delegated directive 2017/593 of Directive 2014/65/EU (MiFID II)
European Union
This delegated directive clarifies that sustainability factors and sustainability related objectives should be taken into account in the product oversight and governance process. It requires investment firms to identify at a sufficiently granular level the potential target market for each financial instrument based upon, amongst other things, a client’s sustainability related objectives. It also states that the sustainability factors of the financial instrument shall be presented in a transparent manner and provide distributers with the relevant information to duly consider any sustainability related objectives of the client or potential client. The proposal was finalised by the Commission on 21 April 2021 and published in the Official Journal in August 2021.

Amendment to Directive 2010/43/EC of Directive 2009/65/EC (UCITS)
European Union
This delegated directive states management companies of UCITS must clarify how they consider sustainability risks and ensure they have the necessary resources and expertise to consider such risks effectively. European Union
This directive aims to improve corporate tax disclosures. It requires multinational companies above a certain turnover and/or employee threshold, with a presence in the EU, through headquarters (HQ), subsidiaries or branches, to publicly disclose income tax information for each member state as well as the EU black and grey lists of non-cooperative jurisdictions for tax purposes. Coporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD)
European Union
This Directive aims to “foster sustainable and responsible corporate behaviour and to anchor human rights and environmental considerations in companies’ operations and corporate governance”. The proposal introduces mandatory environmental and human rights due diligence requirements across global value chains and some director’s duties, including a duty of care to “take into account decisions on sustainability matters”, a duty to set up and oversee due diligence, and a duty to adopt transition plans and link these plans to variable remuneration. Directive (EU) 2016/2341 (Revised Institutions for Occupational Retirement Provision Directive (IORP II))
European Union
This revised directive (replacing Directive 2003/41/EC which is no longer in force) requires pension funds above a certain size to consider ESG factors and disclose how their risks are considered in the Investment Policy Statement.
More precisely, it requires all IORPs to have in place (and regularly review) an effective system of governance which includes consideration of ESG factors related to investment assets in investment decisions. It also requires IORPs to have in place an effective risk-management function which covers, where appliable, ESG risks relating to the investment portfolio and the management thereof. Furthermore, it requires IORPs to carry out and document their own-risk assessment, which includes, where ESG factors are considered in investment decisions, an assessment of new or emerging risks, including risks related to climate change, use of resources and the environment, social risks and risks related to the depreciation of assets due to regulatory change. Every IORP registered or authorised in their territories must prepare and, at least every three years, review a public written statement of investment-policy principles, which contains an explanation of how the investment policy takes ESG factors into account. Finally, it requires IORPs to ensure that prospective members are informed about how ESG and climate factors are considered in the investment approach.
This revised directive came into effect on 12 January 2017 and Member States had two years from then to transpose it into national law.

Directive 2003/87/EC (Emissions Trading Scheme, ETS)
European Union
This directive defines the EU Emissions Trading system. This cap and trade system for emissions operates in all EU countries (plus Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway), limiting emissions from power stations, industrial plants and airlines operating between these countries. It covers around 45% of the EU's greenhouse gas emissions. The EU ETS has developed in phases. The legislative framework for phase 4 (2021-2030) was revised in early 2018 (under directive (EU) 2018/410) to enable it to achieve the EU's 2030 emission reduction targets and as part of the EU's contribution to the Paris Agreement. The system covers carbon dioxide (from power and heat generation; energy-intensive industry sectors including oil refineries, steel works and production of all metals, cement, lime, glass, ceramics, pulp, paper, cardboard, acids and bulk organic chemicals; and commercial aviation), nitrous oxide (from production of nitric, adipic and glyoxylic acids and glyoxal) and perfluorocarbons (PFCs) from aluminium production.
Participation in the EU ETS is mandatory for companies in these sectors, but in some sectors only plants above a certain size are included. Certain small installations can be excluded if governments put in place fiscal or other measures that will cut their emissions by an equivalent amount. In the aviation sector, until 31 December 2023 the EU ETS will apply only to flights between airports located in the European Economic Area (EEA). In 2021, as part of their ‘Fit for 55’ package, the Commission proposed two directives to revise the current EU ETS directive. The first, to lower the overall emission cap even further and increase its annual rate of reduction. The second, to phase out free emission allowances for aviation and align with the global Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation (CORSIA) and to include shipping emissions for the first time in the EU ETS. To address the lack of emissions reductions in road transport and buildings, a separate new emissions trading system is set up for fuel distribution for road transport and buildings. The Commission states that, to complement the substantial spending on climate in the EU budget, Member States should spend the entirety of their emissions trading revenues on climate and energy-related projects. A dedicated part of the revenues from the new system for road transport and buildings should address the possible social impact on vulnerable households, micro-enterprises and transport users.

Directive 2007/36/EC (Shareholder Rights Directive)
European Union
This Directive aims to ensure that shareholders have timely access to relevant information ahead of general meetings by requiring the personal data of shareholders to be processed to enable the company to identify its existing shareholders. This allows companies to communicate directly with them, with a view to facilitating the exercise of shareholder rights and shareholder engagement. The revised directive, as of 2017, also sets out disclosure obligations for institutional investors, asset managers and proxy advisors and further requirements for company directors. The revision aims to prevent shareholders exerting short-term pressure on investee companies, at the expense of longer-term value creation and ESG issues. Amongst other requirements, institutional investors and asset managers must establish and publicly disclose an engagement and voting policy.

Directive (EU) 2014/95 (Non-Financial Reporting Directive, NFRD)
European Union
This directive (which amends the Accounting Directive 2013/34/EU) aims to provide high quality, relevant and comparable data on how companies operate and manage social and environmental challenges, and thus to aid investors, consumers, policy makers and other stakeholders to analyse sustainability performance of large companies and to persuade these companies to develop a responsible approach to business.
It applies to public financial and non-financial companies with over 500 employees, who must disclose, as part of their annual reports, their implementation of policies relating to environmental protection; social responsibility and treatment of employees; respect for human rights; anti-corruption and bribery; and diversity on company boards (in terms of age, gender, educational and professional background). EU member states were required to transpose the rules of the EU non-financial reporting directive by 1 December 2016. As part of the its Communication on the European Green Deal in December 2019, the Commission committed to review the non-financial reporting directive in 2020. The consultation was closed in June and a revised version of the NFRD was proposed by the Commission on 21 April 2021 - known as the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD). The proposed CSRD has a wider scope, requires audit, introduces more detailed reporting requirements and requires companies to digitally "tag" the information. The detailed reporting requirements will be determined by European Sustainability Reporting Stdards (ESRS) currently under development by EFRAG. The CSRD is expected to enter into force with the first set of ESRS on 1st January 2024.
